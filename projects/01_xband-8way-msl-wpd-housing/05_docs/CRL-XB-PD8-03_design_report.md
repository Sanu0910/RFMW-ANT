## Why the lid has a cage

An empty 99.5 x 127.1 mm cavity has its first box mode (TE110) at **1.91 GHz** and dozens more inside 8-12 GHz, so the
divider would show deep notches and poor isolation. The lid therefore carries a solid block that fills the cavity and
rests on the board through a conductive gasket. Each line runs in its own milled channel 2.5 mm from the copper.

Design history: with the channel walls on the pour edge (4.5 mm from the copper, the obvious choice) the merged
channels around the Wilkinson resistor sections were up to ~14 mm wide and resonated at 10.6-10.7 GHz, inside the band.
Moving the walls to 2.5 mm pushed the lowest mode above 15.9 GHz.

4. The east-wall seam between output flanges has no lid screw (bores and flange threads leave no room): largest gap
   30.5 mm. It is backed by the cage block (0.1 mm labyrinth, 6.6 mm deep) and the gasket; keep the lid seat flat to 0.03.
